How this works

Predictions are grouped into bins, by default one per stated probability. For each bin the plot shows the stated probability against the share of its predictions that came true (the red line).

The thin lines are simulated worlds. In each one, every prediction comes true with exactly its stated probability, so they show how far a perfectly calibrated forecaster's record would still wander from the diagonal by chance, given how many predictions each bin holds. A bin outside the middle 95% of worlds (or the share you set) is marked with a triangle pointing the way it is out.

Three summaries are compared with the same worlds:

  • Weighted calibration error: the squared gap between realized frequency and stated probability, averaged over bins with each bin weighted by its share of the predictions.
  • Unweighted calibration error: the same squared gap, averaged over bins equally.
  • Log loss: minus the sum of the natural log of the probability given to what actually happened. Lower is better; always saying 50% scores n log 2.

Each p-value is the share of simulated worlds whose value is strictly larger than yours. A small p-value means a calibrated forecaster would rarely do as badly. For log loss, a p-value close to 1 means less loss than calibrated forecasts at these probabilities usually incur, which is what underconfidence looks like.

Stated probabilities of exactly 0% or 100% are not accepted: if such a forecast is wrong its log loss is infinite. Use 1% or 99% instead.

The seed fixes the simulated worlds, so the same predictions and settings always give the same numbers. Change it to see how much the results move by chance; more simulated worlds make them move less.

The analysis is also available to scripts and agents: see the API description.

Paste or edit as CSV/TSV

One line per prediction, after a header line naming the columns text (optional), probability and outcome, in any order. Probabilities are written 0.7 or 70%; outcomes 1 or 0, true or false, yes or no. Commas or tabs both work, so cells pasted from a spreadsheet are fine.
