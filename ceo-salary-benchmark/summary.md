# Leadership compensation benchmark

This directory contains a validated public-record CEO compensation benchmark for Rethink Priorities, a separately reviewed Form 990 layer for other leadership and functional positions, and a standalone vanilla HTML/CSS/JavaScript explorer. CEO is canonical at `https://vetr.dev/ceo-salary-benchmark/`; supported positions receive equivalent semantic routes such as `/coo-salary-benchmark/`. These routes are deliberately absent from the vetr.dev homepage project list.

## Web application

`index.html`, `style.css`, `app.js`, and generated `app-data.js` form the static GitHub Pages application. The desktop layout uses a settings column, a central weighted histogram and results view, and a sortable/filterable evidence table. Hover-revealed, keyboard-accessible dividers resize both outer columns and the chart/results split; normalized sizes persist locally without entering shared analysis URLs. The histogram and scatterplot redraw from the chart container's measured width and height, so changing a panel or browser zoom reflows rather than proportionally shrinking a fixed-aspect SVG. Divider bounds account for chart headings and legends, keeping every chart child inside its panel even at the supported short-height breakpoint. The Results panel is content-sized until a user explicitly resizes it. At intermediate widths the table moves below the analysis and its divider disappears; narrow layouts stack every region and remove all dividers. The position name in the page title is a deliberately stealthy native selector: it defaults to CEO, exposes 14 standardized non-CEO titles with at least 15 default observations across at least 12 organizations, and signals interactivity through its pointer cursor, dark-blue hover state, and keyboard focus outline. The supported titles include COO, CFO, Chief of Staff, Vice President levels, named director roles, Finance Director, and Senior Researcher. The source-native title remains visible, while aliases such as COO/Chief Operating Officer are consolidated without merging different seniority levels or broad functions. The page title, chart terminology, RP reference marks, compensation choices, filters, weights, quantiles, and source previews follow the active position. The native menu displays each option's default analytical sample as `(n = …)` while the closed title shows only the position label.

The default All sources view pools incumbent Schedule J base pay with job-posting midpoint, both in July 2026 USD. Users can switch to either pay source alone, and Form 990 users can also select validated Part VII reported cash pay or total-reported-pay estimates. The combined view does not pool all three filing measures because that would duplicate correlated records from the same executive and falsely inflate precision. Pay source remains available as a filter, table column, scatterplot color variable, and optional 50/50 balancing rule.

The acquisition manifest contains 353 archived files—175 supporting web pages, 136 Form 990s (135 peer filings plus RP's filing-only reference), 34 job-ad files, 7 frozen inputs, and 1 documented search record—across 200 named organizations. Many files support peer discovery, metadata, validation, or documented exclusion; some organizations have multiple files. The default Recommended peers histogram instead contains 129 pay records: 114 validated incumbent base-pay records plus 15 quantitative job postings. These represent 126 unique organizations because three organizations occur once in each pay source. The RP reference remains outside every analytical sample. Alternative measures, axes, and scatterplots may contain fewer marks because rows without every selected value cannot be plotted. The archive count is documented here and in the acquisition manifest rather than displayed in the app header.

The default view is an equally weighted lognormal summary with its density overlaid on the histogram; empirical and gamma summaries are explicit alternatives. The fitted density has a white halo for contrast against colored blocks. When a fitted model and fewer than 21 quantile levels are selected, checked-by-default curve marks identify modeled percentiles and formatted axis values; empirical mode instead draws full-height dashed percentile guides behind the blocks. Both fitted and empirical percentile/value pairs use measured glyph bounds and the current SVG-to-screen scale to compensate for the font cell's invisible leading. Fitted labels add a quarter-line-height separation so their rotated glyphs and white outlines do not clip, while preserving their curve-normal offset and local center; empirical labels retain their compact horizontal stack and pass through an interval-selection step that keeps the largest non-overlapping set while preferring percentiles near the median. The fitted-model tick keeps a continuous dark crossing while its white halo is split around the density line. The 2–200 bar control is initialized to make blocks approximately square at the current viewport. Exact-record rug marks are always present, and hovering or focusing a block emphasizes its corresponding rug. RP is a display-only favicon marker and never enters bars or fits. Tooltips show the active empirical, lognormal, or gamma percentile as a compact value followed by an unweighted tied midrank count such as `42.3 (#53 / 129)`; changing Distribution immediately changes the percentile while preserving the rank context. They also report the chart coordinate, advertised range where applicable, peer group, pay year/source, similarity score, effective weight, and active color category. Histogram and scatterplot share peer-group, mission-area, connection-to-effective-altruism, pay-source, job-title, and organization-type color options. Responsive controls and viewport-based chart rows keep the visualization usable under browser zoom. Job-posting colors consolidate exact source-native subgroups into the board-facing closest, broader, and broadest peer groups; exact taxonomy codes remain in detailed provenance only.

The dollar-basis control switches the chart, fitted distribution, quantiles, salary filter, previews, and shareable analytical state between inflation-adjusted July 2026 USD and nominal source-year dollars. The table keeps both measures visible simultaneously: `2026 Adj. Salary` shows the adjusted analytical observation, while `Reported Salary` shows source-year Form 990 base compensation or the exact source-advertised recruitment range. Reported Salary sits immediately before Source; both salary columns sort on their corresponding scalar analytical value, with recruitment ranges sorting by midpoint. Because the chart's salary scale changes, switching basis resets the salary range to its new full extent while preserving any unrelated expense-range filter. Adjusted values follow BLS CPI-U for all urban consumers, U.S. city average, all items, not seasonally adjusted (CUUR0000SA0), targeting the July 2026 index of 333.918. Form 990 compensation uses the annual-average CPI for its IRS compensation calendar year; recruitment evidence uses posting-month CPI where available. Source previews preserve the verbatim nominal evidence text in both modes and state the active basis, CPI factor, and reference period.

The histogram horizontal axis and both scatterplot axes are independently selectable by clicking the plain in-chart label or its vertically centered blue triangle. Available measures are salary, expenses, revenue, staff, match score, evidence year, and every supported standardized-position salary. Position values are matched only when the same Form 990 filing has exactly one default-eligible counterpart for that standardized title; ambiguous multi-holder matches remain missing. This supports expressions such as CEO salary / COO salary without joining across organizations or years. Each visible axis has independent Measure/Ratio and Linear/Log controls, so one scatter axis can remain a measure while the other is a ratio. Ratios divide separately selected positive numerators and denominators. A newly selected expression activates the recommended scale—log for ratios, expenses, revenue, and staff; linear for salary, match score, and evidence year—after which the user may override it. Labels show the underlying quantity and units without adding misleading “log transformed” text. Log histograms use equal-width intervals in log coordinates and transform fitted densities with the change-of-variable Jacobian, keeping blocks, curves, quantiles, rugs, RP guides, and raw-value ticks on one mathematical scale. Rows missing any required axis operand remain selected but are omitted from the current plot, and plotted weights are renormalized to mean one. Such rows remain checked and preserve their saved user multiplier, but are faded in place rather than consuming a dedicated eligibility column; an accessibility description retains the exact missing-value reason. The quantile table follows the histogram or scatter-vertical expression and model. Scatter tooltips report a distribution-sensitive percentile and compact rank context for both axes. A one-line header reports weighted Pearson and weighted rank-based Spearman correlations to three decimals. Point area is linear in normalized weight, capped at ten times the base area, and accompanied by a weight-size legend when weighting is active. Optional weighted bivariate-normal 50%, 80%, and 95% covariance ellipses are descriptive data contours, not causal or parameter-confidence intervals. RP can extend the displayed axes so its marker remains visible but is never included in peer fits, quantiles, correlations, contours, weights, or counts.

The Sample control offers Recommended peers, Recommended + broader, Similar organization types, Closest peers only, and All usable salaries. Each choice has a short plain-language description; the Pay source control independently chooses Form 990 records, job postings, or both. Persistently visible settings and table labels stay compact—for example, Inflation Adj., Original USD, Title, Score, Focus Area, Effective Altruism, and Quantiles—while help text and provenance retain the detail needed to interpret them. Primary controls, filters, legends, table cells, chart tooltips, and weighting editors use board-facing category names such as closest, broader, and broadest peers. Exact historical taxonomy codes remain available only in the detailed evidence and provenance views.

Weighting is component-based: users may combine expense-size similarity, staff-size similarity, record age, peer group, connection to effective altruism, pay source, mission area, job-title category, and organization type. Automatic weights is a visually separated, mutually exclusive alternative with two pay-blind source-specific rules. Form 990 rows use a Gaussian similarity kernel over robust-standardized log expenses and staff; it uses RP's same-definition filing anchors ($20.379M expenses and 43 employees), reduces records with missing scale values, and adaptively chooses the narrowest bandwidth reaching the selected effective sample size while keeping the largest normalized filing-kernel weight at or below six. If too few filing rows remain, they receive equal weights rather than causing a calibration failure. Job postings use their reviewed non-pay similarity scores divided by the selected eligible posting mean and bounded to 0.50–1.50 while preserving mean weight one; advertised salary never enters that rule. The separate 50/50 pay-source balance is optional and is applied after Automatic weights. Selecting an individual component turns Automatic weights off to avoid double-counting the same judgment. The opaque component checklist sticks at the top of the desktop settings scroller with a small white inset and lets the dynamic explanation scroll beneath it. Active component multipliers and any user-edited row multiplier are multiplied together, after which all positive plot-eligible rows in the filtered analysis sample are normalized to mean weight 1. Plot-ineligible rows neither influence automatic calibration nor organization/source balancing. Automatic table weight cells update with the current plot's normalized total; explicitly edited cells remain visibly marked and retain their user multiplier while temporarily ineligible. Every discrete component opens with documented suggested values and plain-language category explainers; uncategorized records remain neutral where penalizing missing metadata would be misleading. Suggested values are initialized on first activation, remain directly editable, and reset only with the global Reset control. Each continuous component's compact response curve follows its target/bandwidth, target effective sample size, or record-age control. Expense and staff plots include RP reference lines. Organization-specific multipliers and row inclusion toggles remain available. Rows missing a ratio operand or scatter coordinate remain selected and visible in the table but are faded and excluded from the current plot. Each chart mark focuses its evidence row.

The RP size defaults are source-backed rather than guessed. Expense similarity targets RP's published $7.5 million 2026 core budget, which excludes the expenses of fiscally sponsored Special Projects. Staff similarity uses RP's published operating scale of 57 full-time equivalents from its archived funding-needs page. Because that FTE figure is not identical to Form 990 Part I, line 5, the app labels it as a weighting target and keeps the filing-comparable table field separate. A dark-blue Rethink Priorities reference row is always the first table row and remains frozen directly below the sticky header while the evidence scrolls. It uses RP's 2024 Form 990 (`benchmark/sources/native/form990/202502879349301540_public.xml`) for Marcus Davis's compensation, $20,378,936 total functional expenses, and $20,599,841 revenue, and RP's complete public 2023 filing for the most recent usable filing-comparable staff count of 43; the 2024 filing reports zero in that field. Both filings and the operating-headcount page have cached originals. Compensation follows the app's inflation-adjusted/source-year toggle. The row has no inclusion checkbox or analytical weight, opens the same source-evidence dialog used by peer filings, and presents its filing links compactly as sources 1 and 2. The same frozen row supplies the display-only RP marks and their tooltips in both chart modes. These marks use the transparent RP favicon with a thin contour-following white halo rather than a rectangular backing, and remain excluded from distributions, fits, correlations, and contours. The page favicon is a local copy of the icon declared by the official RP website.

Organization and source-native title fields are separate. The Title filter groups exact, indented source titles beneath CEO, Executive Director, President, Co-leadership, Other, and Not-reported family checkboxes rather than replacing their displayed wording. A family checkbox is checked only when all of its exact titles are checked, shows a partial state otherwise, and selects or deselects only its own children. The Focus Area filter applies the same nested interaction to ten broad navigation families while retaining exact analytical categories as children. The data build rejects scale metadata such as budget, staff, revenue, or expenses if it appears in a normalized job-posting focus area; source-native wording remains available in evidence previews. The Title column is compact and wraps long source-native titles, while Staff sits directly after Expenses. The RP reference row uses the same alignment and formatting classes as peer rows. A filter whose values are all checked offers “Deselect all”; partial or empty selections offer “Select all.” Pay source, Title, Peer group, Focus Area, Location, Effective Altruism, and Organization type use checkbox menus that support multi-selection. Only one column-filter popover can be open at once; it closes after a click elsewhere, an Escape keypress, or horizontal scrolling that moves its column outside the viewport, while interactions inside the menu keep it open. Pay source is followed by Reported Salary and the final Source column, whose View button and external Open link share one cell. Each filter funnel shares a compact, vertically centered inline control group with its sort label and stays within its header at narrow widths; header names use at most two lines. Inactive funnels are neutral, active funnels are blue, and each open menu reports the selected count or range. Salary, Expenses, and Score reveal two-ended range controls only while open. All filters update the table, chart, fitted distribution, statistics, and quantiles from the same record subset. The table initially sorts closest peer groups before broader and excluded groups, with organization name as a stable secondary key. Its checkbox column is also sortable: selected and chart-eligible records come first, followed by selected but currently ineligible records, then the corresponding unselected groups. Plot-ineligible rows remain selected but fade visually; their checked checkbox uses grey rather than the active blue. Every sortable column exposes its direction accessibly. Unchecked records with usable pay explain why they are outside the recommended group and remain manually includable; records without a usable pay measure explain that separate limitation and cannot be included. Dotted missing-metadata cells explain why Effective Altruism or Organization type is unclassified rather than treating missing evidence as a substantive finding. The settings column clips horizontal overflow while retaining its independent vertical scroller, so expanding weight controls never introduces a horizontal scrollbar.

The table also exposes staff count and evidence year. Preserved Form 990 website declarations and audited overrides supply organization-homepage links for 128 incumbent peers. Hovering or focusing the organization-name text—not unused space elsewhere in its cell—opens a concise Wikipedia card without repeating table metadata. `data/organization_wikipedia_profiles.csv` records a complete 193-organization audit across CEO, posting, and public-position rows: 100 mappings use an exact organization title/redirect or a manually reviewed override, while 93 ambiguous or absent articles remain intentionally unmapped. The runtime requests only the mapped article title, never the first search result; unmapped organizations show a neutral unavailable message and a search link. `scripts/audit_wikipedia_profiles.py` can refresh the mapping conservatively, and the app-data build fails on incomplete coverage or an unapproved mapping method. Quantiles can be shown as quintiles, deciles, all percentiles, or user-entered percentile levels, and the panel identifies whether they come from empirical weighted ranks or a fitted distribution. Ordinal suffixes in percentile labels are superscripted. Hovering or focusing a quantile projects it onto the chart. The interface uses the Rethink Priorities blue palette and the local Aspekta variable font. Help tooltips use collision-aware fixed positioning so they remain inside the viewport. Scrollable panels reserve a stable internal gutter and use narrow, semi-transparent scroll thumbs, preventing scrollbar appearance from shifting panel contents.

The lower Results panel has three keyboard-accessible views: Quantiles, Compare, and Robustness. Compare stores up to four named analytical scenarios in versioned browser-local storage. Each scenario is an immutable snapshot of the same quantile-relevant settings encoded in a share link, rather than a copy of presentation-only choices such as bins, colors, table sort, or axis-scale overrides. The comparison table places P25, median, P75, RP's reference value and modeled/empirical percentile, records, organizations, effective sample size, and the principal assumptions side by side; numeric deltas appear only when scenarios describe the same quantity, pay source/measure, and dollar basis. Concise rows disclose active filters, peer overrides, weight edits, and weight parameters. A saved scenario can be applied, updated with the current analysis, renamed, copied as a standalone URL, or removed. Scenario fingerprints canonicalize unordered selections, and applying one is transactional: malformed records are skipped, a failed restore rolls back to the prior analysis, and a successful apply refreshes results against the current data. Presentation choices are preserved only when doing so leaves the saved outcome unchanged. Older-data scenarios suppress numerical comparisons until reapplied and refreshed.

Robustness runs a pure, non-mutating sensitivity evaluator over the selected position's pay, regardless of the currently displayed chart expression. For CEO, the default comparable core crosses four peer samples, three distribution choices, and equal versus automatic weighting, producing 24 specifications; the automatic branch fixes and discloses target effective sample size 35. It reports P25, median, and P75 envelopes, the largest typical median shift by varied dimension, records, organizations, organization-level effective sample size, maximum organization influence, automatic-weight fallback diagnostics, and explicit invalid or small-sample warnings. Specifications fail closed below five organizations, organization-level effective sample size three, or usable dispersion. Collision-aware placement separates coincident marks, while a single nearest-point interaction layer ensures dense marks cannot steal one another's pointer events. Each plotted core result has a viewport-safe hover/focus tooltip describing the exact sample, distribution, weighting, pay source and measure, dollar basis, retained filters, records, organizations, and effective sample size. Clicking it—or using Enter or Space—transactionally applies that salary specification to the main analysis, clears incompatible ratio-axis state and manual sample overrides, reruns Robustness, and preserves keyboard focus; arrow-key roving navigation avoids dozens of tab stops. Four pay-source checks, three same-cohort Form 990 pay-measure checks, three same-posting lower/midpoint/upper checks, and two same-cohort adjusted-versus-original-dollar checks are deliberately shown outside the core envelope because those choices change the evidence, measurement, or units rather than merely the model specification. The dark current-specification marker honors manual row inclusion and active filters, while the source comparison deliberately clears only the pay-source filter it is testing. The display labels every range as sensitivity analysis, not a confidence interval, exposes a textual chart description, and keeps all 36 checks available in an audit table. Other positions omit unsupported source, job-posting, and automatic-weight comparisons.

Quantile-relevant analysis state is shareable through a versioned, compact URL. Position is represented by the semantic path itself—switching COO changes `/ceo-salary-benchmark/` to `/coo-salary-benchmark/`—while a compact base64url `s` parameter appears only when other quantile-affecting settings differ from defaults. `scripts/build_position_routes.py` generates 14 position-specific Jekyll permalink sources under `position-routes/`, keeping every benchmark source inside the canonical app directory while GitHub Pages continues to publish the established root-level URLs. Stable variable tokens support standardized-position operands such as `p:coo` without depending on catalog order. Short field codes and stable hashed row identifiers retain the dollar basis, model, evidence/measure/sample choices, the active histogram or scatter-vertical quantile variable, ratio operands, active weights and nondefault parameters, edited row multipliers, inclusion overrides, analytical filters, numeric ranges, and requested quantile levels. Generated position permalink pages receive the canonical asset base during route generation. ## Multi-position Form 990 layer

`scripts/extract_form990_positions.py` independently parses every Part VII, Section A person from all 136 cached Form 990 XML filings and joins Schedule J only by exact case-insensitive person name. It retains 2,785 deduplicated people and 814 exact Schedule J joins. The reviewed layer contains 884 person-title taxonomy groups and 989 catalog-eligible non-CEO source rows; 778 records pass the compensation-year role test and 755 peer observations enter default position samples. Ordinary current rows are labeled `no_transition_indicated`, which is an absence-of-transition screen rather than independent proof of full-year tenure; separately reviewed later departures can be labeled `verified_full_year`, and roles known to begin after the compensation year remain ineligible. A second exclusive classifier maps each person to at most one standardized title, consolidates equivalent abbreviations, intercepts genuinely hybrid or ambiguous titles, and keeps sparse titles separate rather than merging them into umbrella roles. The public app exposes only the 14 primary standardized non-CEO titles with at least 15 default rows across at least 12 organizations. Including the sensitivity-only LEEP COO addendum, they contain 462 audited standardized-title rows, 420 role-eligible rows, and 409 default observations. Exploratory and hidden sparse titles remain in `form990_benchmark_position_catalog.csv` and `form990_position_observations.csv` for audit, but are intentionally absent from the position selector, cross-position ratio operands, and generated public routes. RP's 2024 filing compensation row is not presented as a COO reference because the official RP report dates Carolyn Footitt's permanent COO appointment to 2025; that transition evidence remains archived and auditable rather than being converted into a false 2024 COO benchmark.

Non-CEO analysis keeps four filing measures distinct. Schedule J base is exact when reported but threshold-selected and incomplete; the coverage-first default is Part VII organization-plus-related reportable cash, which is a compensation proxy rather than exact salary. Part VII total adds other compensation. Nominal source values and July 2026 CPI-U values are both preserved. Because a filing can report several people in one family, automatic non-CEO row weights are balanced within organization before user multipliers are applied, so each selected organization has equal total default influence. The Form 990 tables are not employee censuses: officer, key-employee, and highest-paid-employee reporting thresholds make every non-CEO distribution upward-selected.

The source-native person and title fields are never overwritten. Each row records them alongside a reviewed effective identity/title when a Form 990 spills a title into the person-name field, Schedule J supplies the exact untruncated title, or an official organization source expands an organization-specific acronym. Each row also records its standardized benchmark title and rule, alias quality, hybrid status/reason, broad functional tags, seniority/title group, role scope, incumbency status, classification confidence, source locator, and inclusion reason. Source rows appear in only one title benchmark. The broader taxonomy contains 884 grouped review outcomes: 555 rule-assigned single-family groups, 70 rule-assigned multi-role groups, 137 reviewed observation overrides, and 122 non-public groups requiring further review. Twenty-six non-obvious classifications have hash-pinned official organization pages or publications; their cached and canonical links appear in the source-preview provenance section beside the standardized-position methodology and catalog. The focused recovery adds one strict COO, one CFO, two Managing Director rows, one Senior Vice President, one General Counsel, one Finance Director, and one Communications Director, while correctly moving three EVP aliases out of ordinary Vice President. Missing roles remain null: the current filings contain no default Operations-family row for 85 of 135 peer organizations and no default Finance-family row for 84, because Form 990 is a threshold-selected disclosure rather than an employee census. The deterministic extractor asserts all filing identities, arithmetic, exact joins, raw-field preservation, inclusion boundaries, known source-internal discrepancies, reviewed effective-title mappings, exact standardized-title counts, supporting hashes, and final counts before it writes the data.

`benchmark/analysis/auto_weight_models/` preserves a reproducible Python and R predictive audit of weighting alternatives. Grouped nested cross-validation on the 110-row Schedule J sample found essentially no out-of-sample gain from the frozen comparability score alone (log-RMSE 0.376 versus 0.379 for the training-fold mean), while expenses plus staff improved log-RMSE to 0.344 and a regularized partial-pooling feature set reached 0.320. Salary-supervised category kernels improved distributional CRPS but used the full effective-sample-size budget, under-covered their nominal intervals, and depended on sparse or singular category effects. The production app therefore uses the transparent outcome-free filing scale kernel and a separate bounded frozen match-score rule for recruitment postings; learned salary-supervised taxonomy weights remain out of production because predictive association is not a normative peer-selection rule and is not stable enough for a board default.
