# CEO salary benchmark

This directory contains a validated public-record CEO compensation benchmark for Rethink Priorities and a standalone vanilla HTML/CSS/JavaScript explorer intended for `https://vetr.dev/ceo-salary-benchmark/`. The route is deliberately absent from the vetr.dev homepage project list.

## Web application

`index.html`, `style.css`, `app.js`, and generated `app-data.js` form the static GitHub Pages application. The desktop layout uses a settings column, a central weighted histogram and quantile view, and a sortable/filterable evidence table. Narrow layouts stack these regions.

The default All evidence view pools incumbent Schedule J base salary with recruitment-posting midpoint, both in July 2026 USD. Users can switch to either source stream alone, and incumbent-only users can also select validated Part VII cash/W-2 or filing-total proxies. The combined view does not pool all three filing measures because that would duplicate correlated observations from the same executive and falsely inflate precision. Evidence type remains available as a filter, table column, scatterplot color variable, and optional 50/50 stream-balancing rule.

The acquisition manifest contains 350 archived files—174 supporting web pages, 136 Form 990s (135 peer filings plus RP's filing-only reference), 32 job-ad files, 7 frozen inputs, and 1 documented search record—across 200 named organizations. Many files support peer discovery, metadata, validation, or documented exclusion; some organizations have multiple files. The default histogram instead contains the 110 validated incumbent base-salary observations plus 15 quantitative recruitment postings that pass the primary preset. The RP reference remains outside every analytical sample. Scatterplots may contain fewer points—for example, the default expense-axis scatter has 117—because rows without the selected horizontal-axis value cannot be plotted. The archive count is documented here and in the acquisition manifest rather than displayed in the app header.

The default view is an equally weighted lognormal summary with its density overlaid on the histogram; empirical and gamma summaries are explicit alternatives. The fitted density has a white halo for contrast against colored blocks. When a fitted model and fewer than 21 quantile levels are selected, checked-by-default curve marks identify modeled percentiles and their rounded dollar values with separately positioned label lines; a collision pass prioritizes percentiles nearest the median and suppresses any annotation whose rendered label would overlap a retained neighbor. Users can disable these annotations. Each orthogonal tick retains a continuous dark stroke across the fitted curve, while only its white halo is split around the intersection; because the tick is rendered after the density, the density halo cannot erase it. The initial 2–200 bin control sits directly below the histogram/scatterplot selector and is calibrated to make organization blocks approximately square at the current viewport. The histogram always shows exact-observation rug marks, reserves headroom above the tallest mark, and labels both axes. Short horizontal-axis tick marks are omitted so they do not resemble rug observations; hovering or focusing a histogram block emphasizes its exact corresponding rug mark. The mark tooltip identifies the organization and role, presents compensation prominently, and reports the bin or scatter coordinate, advertised range where applicable, peer tier, evidence year and type, match score, effective weight, and active color category. Sidebar controls switch between histogram and scatterplot and apply a shared color field—tier, topic/model, EA relationship, evidence stream, job-title group, or structure—to either view. Responsive radio groups keep labels inside their controls, and viewport-based chart rows prevent browser zoom from leaving an oversized, stale chart. For color encoding, the recruitment taxonomy is consolidated into readable Tier A / primary, Tier B / secondary, and Tier C / expanded legend groups; exact source-native subtiers remain visible in the table and tooltip. Visible descriptions and collision-aware help explain the category codings, including the tier hierarchy.

The dollar-basis control switches the entire salary analysis between inflation-adjusted July 2026 USD and nominal source-year dollars. It changes table salaries, histogram and scatterplot values, fitted distributions, quantiles, salary filters, chart and preview labels, advertised posting ranges, and shareable URL state together. Because the salary scale changes, switching basis resets the salary range to its new full extent while preserving any unrelated expense-range filter. Adjusted values follow BLS CPI-U for all urban consumers, U.S. city average, all items, not seasonally adjusted (CUUR0000SA0), targeting the July 2026 index of 333.918. Form 990 compensation uses the annual-average CPI for its IRS compensation calendar year; recruitment evidence uses posting-month CPI where available. Unadjusted recruitment values use the midpoint of the original advertised range, matching the analytical measure rather than either range endpoint. Source previews preserve the verbatim nominal evidence text in both modes and state the active basis, CPI factor, and reference period.

The scatterplot places salary on the vertical axis and expenses, revenue, staff, comparability score, or evidence year on the horizontal axis. A single comma-separated summary at the far left of its control header reports weighted Pearson and weighted rank-based Spearman correlations on the displayed axis scales to three decimals, keeping statistics outside the plotting area. Point area is linear in normalized total weight and capped at ten times the unweighted reference area. Whenever analytical or row-specific weights are active, the legend adds 0.5×, 1.0×, and 2.0× reference circles whose areas follow that mapping. Optional weighted bivariate-normal covariance ellipses show approximately 50%, 80%, and 95% data contours; they are descriptive data ellipses, not causal or parameter-confidence intervals.

Sample presets are described in place: the validated primary set follows the source and role-structure audit; the structurally clean subset additionally removes flagged structures; Tier A / strict-primary is the narrowest peer definition; and every observed salary includes broader sensitivity rows. Weighting is component-based: users may combine expense similarity, staff similarity, recency, tier, EA relationship, evidence stream, topic/model, title group, and structure. Auto-weights is a visually separated, mutually exclusive alternative because its frozen 0–100 composite peer score already combines function, expenses, staff, EA affinity, executive structure, and geography; selecting it clears the individual components and prevents accidental double-counting. Its explainer sits beside the title of the unindented response panel that appears only when Auto-weights is active. The separate Equalize evidence streams rule rescales Form 990s and recruitment postings after other weights so each contributes 50% of total analytical weight; it is available only in the combined view, remains compatible with Auto-weights, and is visually distinct from row-level components. The opaque component checklist sticks at the top of the desktop settings scroller with a small white inset, fully covers earlier controls, and lets the dynamic base-weight description scroll beneath it. Active component multipliers and any user-edited row multiplier are multiplied together, after which all positive rows in the filtered analysis sample are normalized to mean weight 1. Automatic table weight cells update with that normalized total; explicitly edited cells remain visibly marked and retain their user multiplier. Every discrete component opens with documented suggested category values: 1.00 is the reference, lower values moderately downweight broader tiers, weaker EA or topical fit, advertised rather than realized compensation, less directly comparable titles, and structurally dissimilar organizations. Every category row has a keyboard-accessible, collision-aware explainer grounded in the frozen non-pay protocol: tier explainers state the score, function, scale, role, and structural rules; EA explainers distinguish explicit EA-core, EA-adjacent/recommended-cause, functional-only, and uncoded relationships; structure explainers decode the source-derived legal, governance, and reporting arrangements. Uncoded categories remain neutral where penalizing missing metadata would be misleading. Suggested values are initialized on first activation, remain directly editable when a component is toggled off and on, and return to their defaults only with the global reset. Each continuous component's compact response curve follows that component's own target/bandwidth or half-life controls, so adjustments and their implications remain visually grouped. Every response plot has four ticks per axis and explicit variable and multiplier labels. Expense and staff plots include a directly labeled RP reference line at $7.5M and 43 filing-reported employees; if the user changes the weighting target, the fixed RP marker remains visible separately. Organization-specific multipliers and row inclusion toggles remain available. Rows without the active compensation measure are consistently excluded from both the chart and table. Each chart mark focuses the corresponding evidence row.

The RP size defaults are source-backed rather than guessed. Expense similarity targets RP's published $7.5 million 2026 core budget, which excludes the expenses of fiscally sponsored Special Projects. Staff similarity targets the 43 individuals employed reported on RP's 2023 Form 990, Part I, line 5—the same field used for peer counts. RP's 2024 return reports zero in that field, so the app explicitly uses the most recent nonzero comparable filing value and identifies its one-year lag. A dark-blue Rethink Priorities reference row is always the first table row and remains frozen directly below the sticky header while the evidence scrolls. It uses RP's 2024 Form 990 (`benchmark/sources/native/form990/202502879349301540_public.xml`) for Marcus Davis's compensation, $20,378,936 total functional expenses, and $20,599,841 revenue, and RP's complete public 2023 filing for staff. Both filings have cached originals. Compensation follows the app's inflation-adjusted/source-year toggle. The row has no inclusion checkbox or analytical weight, opens the same source-evidence dialog used by peer filings, and links to both filings. The page favicon is a local copy of the icon declared by the official RP website.

Organization and source-native job-title fields are separate. The title filter groups exact, indented source titles beneath clickable CEO, Executive Director, President, Co-leadership, Other, and Not-reported headings rather than replacing their displayed wording. A filter whose values are all checked offers “Deselect all”; partial or empty selections offer “Select all.” Evidence type, title, tier, topic, location, EA relationship, and organizational-structure columns use exact-value checkbox menus that support multi-selection. All filter affordances are icon-sized funnels integrated to the right of their sortable labels in the table's single header row: inactive funnels are neutral, active funnels are blue, and the expanded menu reports the selected count or active range. Salary and Expenses reveal two-ended range controls only while their popovers are open; the expense control uses a logarithmic scale. Categorical and numeric filters update the table, chart, fitted distribution, statistics, and quantiles from the same observation subset. The global table search and invariant work-model column are omitted. The table initially sorts from the narrowest peer tier to broader, sensitivity, and excluded tiers, with organization name as a stable secondary key. Every sortable column shows a persistent sort affordance and exposes its active direction accessibly, while the compact Tier column truncates long labels with their full value available on hover.

The table also exposes staff count and evidence year. Preserved Form 990 website declarations supply organization-homepage links for 121 incumbent peers. Hovering or focusing the organization-name text—not unused space elsewhere in its cell—opens a concise Wikipedia card without repeating table metadata. `data/organization_wikipedia_profiles.csv` records a complete 174-organization audit: 98 mappings use an exact organization title/redirect or a manually reviewed override, while 76 ambiguous or absent articles remain intentionally unmapped. The runtime requests only the mapped article title, never the first search result; unmapped organizations show a neutral unavailable message and a search link. `scripts/audit_wikipedia_profiles.py` can refresh the mapping conservatively, and the app-data build fails on incomplete coverage or an unapproved mapping method. Quantiles can be shown as quintiles, deciles, all percentiles, or user-entered percentile levels, and the panel identifies whether they come from empirical weighted ranks or a fitted distribution. Ordinal suffixes in percentile labels are superscripted. Hovering or focusing a quantile projects it onto the chart. The interface uses the Rethink Priorities blue palette and the local Aspekta variable font. Help tooltips use collision-aware fixed positioning so they remain inside the viewport. Scrollable panels reserve a stable internal gutter and use narrow, semi-transparent scroll thumbs, preventing scrollbar appearance from shifting panel contents.

Quantile-relevant analysis state is shareable through one versioned, compact, base64url query parameter. The third schema makes All evidence and inflation-adjusted dollars the omitted defaults while retaining short field codes and stable hashed row identifiers for the dollar basis, model, evidence/measure/sample choices, active weights and nondefault parameters, edited row multipliers, inclusion overrides, analytical filters, numeric ranges, and requested quantile levels. Histogram/scatter view, bins, color, contour visibility, curve-mark visibility, and table sorting are deliberately excluded. Legacy version-one and version-two links remain readable with their original defaults. The page restores links without network storage, uses `history.replaceState` as settings change, removes state when analysis settings are at their defaults, and visibly falls back to defaults if a malformed or unsupported link is opened.

Source previews display the exact evidence fields used for the selected measure. Third-party links open the public host, while `evidence/original/` contains compactly published copies of the original XML, HTML, or PDF artifacts referenced by app observations. `scripts/build_app_data.py` rebuilds both the app dataset and this publishable source subset from the validated benchmark deliverables.

The preserved category-explainer package under `benchmark/deliverables/category_explainers/` adds 270 exact-value definitions and 312 row-level provenance records: 144 selected-reference classifications, 135 Form 990 observation classifications, and 33 job-ad classifications. The data build fails if an app row lacks its applicable selection/job-ad rationale, embeds the matched records into `app-data.js`, and publishes links to the complete dictionary, rationale CSV, methodology, and validation report. Discrete-weight help uses the package's definitions while explicitly identifying the app's suggested multipliers as editable sensitivity judgments rather than source rules. Source previews expose classification timing, provenance type, confidence, caveats, row-level rationales, and local citations. Incumbent rows retain the selected-reference taxonomy used by table filters and charts while separately showing later filing-review classifications; this preserves documented differences such as Social Science Research Council being Tier A in the selected reference set and Tier B in the filing-analysis record rather than silently overwriting either layer. Later descriptive structure flags and non-preregistered expanded job-ad subtype names are labeled as such.

## Analytical package

`benchmark/` contains the protocol chain, frozen peer universes, original analysis, independent validation, source manifests, and acquisition utilities. Stable source IDs connect analytical rows to source-native files and retrieval metadata. All 350 required source records are locally preserved: 136 IRS XML returns, 32 job-ad records, 174 supporting web sources, seven frozen inputs, and one documented search record.

Independent parsing found that the original analysis omitted Schedule J base compensation and misstated the Center for AI Safety Part VII amounts. The app therefore uses `validated_form990_compensation.csv` and `validated_job_ad_compensation.csv`, not the uncorrected original evidence columns. All 15 primary quantitative job-ad ranges now verify locally. The Marine Science Institute exclusion remains unchanged, but its recovered source corrects the role title to Executive Director.

The strict `source_complete` packager remains fail-closed because it requires the original expected fields to agree with every source as well as requiring all files to exist. Its remaining failures are extraction/data-contract findings, not remote-retrieval gaps.

## Reproduction and testing

- `python3 scripts/build_app_data.py` rebuilds the static app data and publishable evidence files.
- `npm run serve` serves the route locally on port 4173.
- `npm test` starts a local server and runs focused Playwright checks for outlined histogram density, collision-decluttered fitted-curve quantile marks, linked block/rug highlighting, CPI-adjusted and nominal salary switching, nominal recruitment midpoints, the sticky RP reference row, shared categorical coloring, distribution switching, the combined evidence stream, scatter axes/colors/covariance contours/correlations/weight-scaled areas, normalized automatic and user-edited weighting, mutually exclusive composite weighting, 50/50 stream balancing, compact URL-state round trips and legacy-link recovery, weight-response curves, sample descriptions, grouped title filtering, select-all/deselect-all state, compact one-row header filters and their active states, y-axis headroom, row inclusion, salary-availability exclusion, filter-to-chart synchronization, two-ended numeric filters, percentile provenance, verified and intentionally unmapped organization previews, H-CAP and Center for AI Safety corrections, viewport-safe tooltips, evidence-stream switching, sticky overlays, zoom-sized charts, and responsive layout.
- The benchmark's independent audits and source validators remain under `benchmark/scripts/`.

The app is decision support for board deliberation, not a population salary estimator or a substitute for a formal compensation-reasonableness process.
