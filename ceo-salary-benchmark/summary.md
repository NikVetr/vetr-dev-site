# Leadership compensation benchmark

This folder contains the public-record compensation benchmark for Rethink Priorities and its static HTML/CSS/JavaScript explorer. CEO is canonical at `/ceo-salary-benchmark/`; 14 other leadership and functional positions have generated semantic routes. The application is decision support for comparisons among reviewed disclosures, not a representative labor-market survey.

## Data and evidence

The empirical Recommended CEO view contains 129 observations across 126 organizations: 112 incumbent Schedule J base salaries and 17 advertised salary midpoints. Cash-pay analysis has 120 default incumbent observations. Rethink Priorities is a display-only reference. CPI converts compensation to July 2026 USD; nominal amounts and exact source measures remain available. Base salary, reported cash, total compensation, and advertised policy ranges remain distinct.

`benchmark/` contains the frozen acquisition and analysis package, subsequent evidence overlays, the multi-position Form 990 extraction, and predictive models. Historical reports describe their own frozen cohorts. `scripts/build_app_data.py` combines validated layers into `app-data.js`, checks source hashes and model provenance, and publishes referenced source files under `evidence/original/`. Large native archives and intermediate fits are ignored by git.

`ceo_reference_set_audit.md`, its row-level CSV in `benchmark/enrichment/`, and `ceo_peer_recommendations.csv` document the salary/scale extraction review and peer-selection recommendations. Copenhagen Consensus Center and ORCID remain eligible: their disclosed pay is supported, although their organizational structures warrant sensitivity analyses. Center for Public Integrity and Nuclear Threat Initiative are off by default and excluded from model training because their reported CEO hours require resolution. They remain selectable empirical sensitivity observations.

The operating-evidence review covers all 201 organizations in two source-linked JSONL files. Current and historical job ads can support explicitly labeled indirect inferences; unknown remains a valid result. CEO hiring market, general hiring scope, and organizational footprint are separate fields. When executive-specific evidence is unavailable, a sufficiently supported staff market is labeled as an inference. RP's CEO is fully remote; its general hiring policy supports conditional international eligibility. Current policy does not establish the arrangement in an earlier pay year.

The follow-up of 57 unresolved work arrangements supports 14 additional combined classifications. Of the 43 still unresolved, 12 have weaker or historical guesses displayed separately from the model input. `unknown_work_arrangements.md` summarizes the evidence, confidence and alternatives; the two follow-up JSONL files retain individual searches.

Work-model dialogs combine current reasoning with dated supporting evidence and saved source copies. Updating a primary citation preserves earlier published evidence; duplicate citations are consolidated. Production Bayesian validation can extend one unsuccessful sampling run while retaining the substantive convergence thresholds, and records each refined fold in the artifact.

`scripts/other_employee_pay.py` selects the highest other-employee pay after converting each eligible annual amount to a 40-hour week: combined filing/related pay × 40 / combined reported weekly hours. This is an effort-based standardization, not verified contractual FTE. The module retains actual pay, hours, source identity, payroll-allocation flags, and base-disclosure completeness alongside the standardized amount. Full-period part-time employees can enter this predictor; former/partial-year roles and unresolved CPI hours cannot. Cash disclosures are never substituted for base pay. `highest_other_fte_audit.md` in the enrichment folder documents the source checks.

The non-CEO layer maps source-native titles through a reviewed taxonomy, retaining original titles and classification evidence. Each organization receives equal initial influence across its selected role holders before user multipliers. Disclosures of senior/high-paid employees cannot establish population pay percentiles for an occupation.

## Application

`index.html`, `style.css`, and `app.js` provide histogram, scatterplot, and CEO-only Model tabs, source previews, editable weights, filters, quantiles, robustness comparisons, and saved scenarios. Compact URLs preserve analytical settings and support undo/redo. Empirical, lognormal, and gamma summaries share internally consistent densities, quantiles, means, and reference percentiles. Position routes are generated by `scripts/build_position_routes.py` and served locally by `scripts/serve_local.py`.

The Model tab uses a fixed, versioned cohort independently of table filtering. Users edit numeric and categorical organization features, choose fractional focus-area weights, and inspect the support for their profile. “Average category effect” means the unweighted centered category effect; a specified mixture is a weighted log effect, not a mixture of different salary distributions.

Model robustness compares P25, median, and P75 across every fitted variant at the same profile. The shared empirical robustness chart provides point inspection, keyboard navigation, model selection, and an expandable result table. Model predictions are cached by variant and profile; input changes refresh the comparison. Switching models preserves supported profile vocabulary even when a selected category has only prior support in the new training cohort. The CV table sorts numeric metrics and separates method, base-only evidence, categorical-input inclusion, other-pay inclusion, and ad inclusion. Exact-base and cash-inclusive Bayesian linear variants share the Bayesian multilevel family name and a Base only sidebar checkbox; ordinary least squares is labeled OLS.

`model-vignettes.js` supplies each model's large, keyboard-accessible specification modal: estimand, equations, priors or penalties, fitting, validation, and uncertainty assumptions. `model-analysis.js` supplies shared spline, kernel, normal-quantile, and Shapley calculations.

`relationship-plots.js` supplies a shared SVG pairs/correlation explorer. The Scatterplot tab offers single-pair and matrix views, selectable numeric features (including current ratio axes), original/log scales, weighted Pearson/Spearman correlations, and either arrangement of mixed triangles. Cells use pairwise-complete records and independently recalibrate the existing weights for that pair; counts and effective sample sizes are available on hover. RP marks are excluded from correlations. Diagonal histograms show marginal distributions. Matrices scale to the available panel or modal viewport beside a persistent, scrollable feature checklist grouped into pay, organization statistics and ratio sections. Fit to screen is the default; Expand details displays the matrix at its intrinsic cell size with scrolling while the checklist remains accessible; cell activation restores the full bivariate plot. Matrix settings persist in shared URLs. Lower-panel summaries continue to describe the selected bivariate axes.

A square Pearson heatmap beneath the driver bars shows only the active profile’s included driver effects, using their aligned joint draws and the selected log/percent units. Its width and alignment match the bar tracks, with abbreviated labels on the left and bottom. Constant effects have undefined correlations. The adjacent Joint uncertainty link opens the full explorer.

Joint driver exploration shows dependencies among profile contributions and selectable categorical effects within one model at a time. Bayesian models use aligned retained posterior draws, linear/GAM models use their joint coefficient approximations, and SVR uses aligned bootstrap fits. GP Shapley contributions use their full conditional covariance, computed from covariances among coalition predictions, with deterministic joint Gaussian simulation. Correlations use every available draw; small pairs use at most 250 evenly spaced joint rows. Display units can be log-salary or percentage contrasts. These are contribution uncertainties, not marginal feature correlations or slopes; a negative contribution can also reflect an input below its reference. Constant contrasts have undefined correlations. No model refitting is needed for these views.

Driver bars show conditional prediction changes from numeric training centers, centered categorical effects, and the functional-overlap EA reference. They are selectable in log units or percentage changes. Clicking a driver opens its posterior or approximate compatibility forest plot, showing all 21 variants by default. The selected model’s rows, interval segments and point markers are outlined, and its labels are bold. Categorical comparisons include every level and the selected-profile contrast, grouped by effect on a shared axis, with model labels on the right. Colors consistently identify model families. Models omitting a categorical predictor are listed once outside the plot. Numeric comparisons retain “Not included” rows for omitted inputs. Each modeled contrast uses that model’s training reference. Values sit just beyond the bars, with explicit effect units. Kernel interactions use exact Shapley allocations over the numeric predictors, with other inputs fixed; these descriptive contrasts are not causal effects.

Predictive quantiles describe variation among comparable peers. A separate interval around each quantile describes estimation uncertainty at a user-selected level, defaulting to 89%. Bayesian models use posterior draws; the Gaussian process conditions on fitted kernel hyperparameters; other models use explicitly labeled coefficient approximations and/or organization bootstrap. Those latter intervals omit some tuning and preprocessing uncertainty.

Bayesian headline percentiles are quantiles of the posterior predictive mixture, including peer variation and parameter uncertainty; GP predictions similarly include latent-function uncertainty conditional on the fitted kernel. Linear/GAM/SVR headline percentiles use a fitted location and residual distribution. Percentile uncertainty intervals instead summarize uncertainty about the conditional population quantile: in the Bayesian model, `exp(mu_draw + sigma_draw * normal_quantile(p))`. At the median this is the posterior of `exp(mu)`; at other percentiles, uncertain peer spread also matters. These intervals represent estimation uncertainty, not an individual salary's predictive range, and need not be centered on the headline predictive percentile. Expected dollar salary is a separate `E(salary)` summary. Model robustness defaults to points and optionally displays these same user-level percentile intervals on separate model rows, with model-family colors and exact endpoints in tooltips.

The sidebar “Uncertainty represents” selector switches between the predictive salary distribution (default) and estimation uncertainty in `exp(mu)`, the exponentiated expected log salary. SVR uses its fitted log-location rather than a conditional-mean claim. The expectation view uses posterior log-location draws, conditional GP latent variance, or joint coefficient/bootstrap location draws, excluding residual peer variation. Draw-based percentiles are calculated on the log scale and exponentiated; kernel smoothing affects only the drawn density. Its cards are quantiles of the estimation distribution, without a second interval around each; the top summary reports the selected central uncertainty interval. Robustness compares median location estimates with optional central intervals. The selector labels distinguish estimation-only uncertainty from estimation plus peer variation; model-specific hover explanations describe posterior, conditional GP, coefficient or bootstrap approximations and the residual-calibration limits. Display notation e^μ explicitly denotes exponentiation of the fitted log-location. The fitted outcome remains log salary in either view. Joint-driver feature lists separate numeric organization statistics, employee pay, profile-category effects and each categorical field’s levels. This target persists in URLs, undo/redo, and saved scenarios; it does not change the fitted models, validation scores, or driver effects.

Form 990 base salary uses Schedule J Part II column B(i), separately from bonuses, other reportable compensation, deferred compensation and nontaxable benefits. Part VII reportable compensation is retained as a cash/W-2 proxy; estimated other compensation is added only for the total-compensation measure. The cash-inclusive Bayesian models explicitly model the proxy-to-base increment; exact-base variants and numeric comparison models use the disclosed base-pay outcomes. Schedule J base is a salary benchmark, but filing conventions for employee deferrals and pretax deductions can differ from contractual gross salary (see the [IRS Schedule J instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i990sj) and [employee-deferral guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/exempt-organization-annual-reporting-requirements-reporting-employee-deferrals-to-401k-and-403b-plans)).

## Predictive analysis

`benchmark/analysis/predictive_salary_models/prepare_model_data.py` prepares 151 records across 147 organization-name groups: 112 exact-base filings, 12 cash proxies, and 27 advertisements. RP never contributes an outcome. The preparation contract records cohort membership, deterministic grouped folds, predictor definitions, and source/code hashes.

`fit_salary_models.R` orchestrates 19 specifications: an intercept baseline; paired OLS, numeric GAM, RBF SVR, and RBF Gaussian-process fits; and eight Bayesian fits crossing linear/additive numeric effects, presence/absence of highest non-CEO pay, and inclusion/exclusion of advertisements. Two exact-base-only Bayesian linear variants retain the multilevel and joint missing-input structure, with/without other pay, while excluding cash-only records and ads. `model_extensions.R` contains spline bases, kernel fits, distribution scores, and uncertainty utilities. `ceo_salary_model.stan` implements the shared multilevel model, joint Gaussian missing inputs, and source-specific pay likelihoods.

`fit_categorical_gam.R` extends the registry to 21 specifications with two frequentist categorical GAMs. `categorical_gam.R` combines the numeric REML cubic smooths with seven penalized one-hot categorical effects, using the same 112 exact-base outcomes and nested grouped calibration as the numeric GAM. Fixed vocabularies retain unobserved levels with uncertainty. Category contrasts and numeric effects share aligned joint coefficient draws, including smoothing-parameter correction, throughout predictions, driver forests, joint matrices and robustness views. Script and training-data hashes guard this incremental fit step. Numeric and categorical GAMs remain separately selectable; their matching predictor fields with Bayesian GAM do not imply identical priors, missing-input models or evidence.

Bayesian additive models regularize natural cubic curvature toward the multilevel linear specification. Both retain partially pooled categories and latent, correlated missing predictors. Cash-only amounts inform latent base pay through a zero-or-positive cash increment; advertisements have a separate offset and scale. Advertised intervals are experimental policy-range evidence, not observed hires.

Every model is evaluated on the same exact-filing outcomes in organization-grouped ten-fold validation. Preprocessing is fitted inside training folds. SVR tuning and deterministic residual calibration are nested. Comparison metrics include log RMSE, out-of-sample R², mean and median absolute percentage error, 80%/90% coverage, log predictive density, and log-scale CRPS. Log scores are densities rather than probabilities; exponentiating a difference in mean log scores gives a geometric density ratio.

`model_artifact.json` exports browser parameters, uncertainty draws, training IDs, diagnostics, and provenance. The build rejects stale inputs, unsupported schemas, missing models, incomplete residual identities, and failed sampler gates. Large fits are cached outside tracked results; optional `prepare_cluster_fits.R` and `cluster_fit_worker.R` support the documented worker cluster with four-chain Stan fits and integrity-checked imports. The model README specifies equations and reproduction details.

`measurement_sensitivity.R` uses those same fitting and scoring functions for three grouped ten-fold repetitions. Matched Bayesian comparisons isolate exact-only training and removal of categorical predictors; single-split sensitivities compare reported other pay and treating incompletely identified maxima as missing. Numeric linear, GAM, SVR and GP comparators use all three splits. The separate `measurement_sensitivity/` results retain every held-out prediction, fold assignment, score and sampler diagnostic. The exact-only with-other-pay app variant uses the first repetition’s held-out fits; the remaining research sensitivities stay separate from the app registry.

`audit_cash_disclosure.py` produces source-linked cash-only records, descriptive cohort summaries, and `cash_disclosure_audit.md`. It compares the same held-out outcomes under pooled and exact-only Bayesian fitting, including the other-pay-missing stratum. Cash-only records have lower observed cash and smaller scale, and all lack the other-pay predictor. Filing disclosure rules and compensation-component measurement are separate issues; the report specifies a disclosure-aware extension without claiming that unobserved cash-only base salaries are validated.

## Reproduction and checks

Run from this folder:

```sh
python3 scripts/build_organization_operating_metadata.py
python3 benchmark/analysis/predictive_salary_models/prepare_model_data.py
Rscript benchmark/analysis/predictive_salary_models/fit_salary_models.R .
Rscript benchmark/analysis/predictive_salary_models/fit_categorical_gam.R .
Rscript benchmark/analysis/predictive_salary_models/measurement_sensitivity.R .
python3 benchmark/analysis/predictive_salary_models/summarize_measurement_study.py
npm run build
python3 benchmark/analysis/predictive_salary_models/audit_cash_disclosure.py
python3 scripts/audit_ceo_reference_set.py
python3 scripts/summarize_work_followup.py
npm run test:data
npm run test:statistics
npm test
```

The model preparation reads the generated app data and applies current review overlays. After changing raw acquisition/extraction layers, rebuild those layers first. A stale model artifact deliberately prevents a production app build; regenerate preparation and fits before publishing. `--quick` is for smoke testing and cannot satisfy production gates. R, CmdStan, and package versions are recorded in the artifact; native evidence files must be present to reproduce source audits.

Python checks validate source contracts, cohort membership, routes, and artifact structure. R/Node checks cover numerical distributions, held-out outcome independence, conditional imputation, spline and GP calculations, and driver decomposition. Playwright tests cover model robustness and variant selection, numeric CV sorting, driver comparisons, control styling, uncertainty, source dialogs, fractional inputs, URL/history behavior, and the broader explorer.

## Interpretation and remaining research

The reference set is a useful broad disclosure cohort with a closer research-peer sensitivity view; it is not an identified sample of RP's hiring market. Avoid deleting organizations because a chosen model predicts them poorly. Priorities are verified additions with comparable leadership scope and disclosed base pay, same-period operating evidence, repeated grouped validation, joint profile-support checks, and sensitivities to disclosure and cash/ad measurement assumptions. A single CV split does not establish a reliable model ranking, and adding nonlinearities does not repair sample selection.
