Funnels, coordinates and the Hastings ratio

Jump to the first figure ↓

A Markov chain Monte Carlo sampler walks through a distribution one step at a time, and in the long run the places it visits are a sample from it. Whether it gets there depends on the distribution's shape in the coordinates the sampler moves in, and on an acceptance rule that is exactly right. Press Play on a panel to watch a chain move; the numbers beside it say how close it has come to the exact answer.

Samplers with fixed settings. These panels use random-walk Metropolis, Gibbs, and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo with the step size and number of steps you set. Chains start at the point shown, with no warm-up or automatic tuning, so every step can be watched. See How it works for the targets and diagnostics.

1 One funnel, three coordinates

Neal's funnel is the shape a hierarchical model takes when the spread between its groups is uncertain: y is normal with SD σ, and given y, x is normal with SD ey. Low y is a narrow neck and high y a wide mouth, so no one step size suits both. Sampled in these coordinates (centred), a chain hardly enters the neck: steps sized for the mouth overshoot it, and there HMC's trajectories diverge. Written as two independent standard normals, with x = xraw eσyraw (non-centred), the same distribution is a round blob that a sampler crosses easily. A third parameterization uses two quantiles in (0, 1) put through Φ⁻¹, sampled on the logit scale. The funnel is untruncated in every parameterization.

2 The Hastings ratio

A Metropolis–Hastings step proposes a move from x to x′ and accepts it with probability min(1, π(x′) q(x | x′) / π(x) q(x′ | x)). When proposals are not symmetric, the ratio q(x | x′)/q(x′ | x) keeps the chain on its target. Multiplying a positive parameter by eλZ, a common move for scales and rates, proposes large values more widely than small ones, and its ratio is x′/x. Leave the ratio out and the chain settles on π(x)/x; turn it upside down and it settles on π(x)/x². Here both coordinates of a log-normal pair are multiplied at each step.

3 A normal and an evil smiley

The correlated normal comes with a Gibbs sampler, which redraws one coordinate at a time from its exact conditional distribution, so every move is accepted but each moves along one axis only. The smiley is a mixture: a banana-shaped mouth with three-quarters of the mass and two small eyes above it, which a sampler has to find and travel between.

How it works

The samplers

Random-walk Metropolis proposes a move by a normal step with the SD you set, in both coordinates at once, and accepts it with probability min(1, π(x′)/π(x)). HMC draws a standard normal momentum, follows the target's gradient by the leapfrog method with step size ε for the number of steps you set, and accepts the end point with probability e−ΔH, the change in total energy; when that change passes 1000 (Stan's threshold), the transition is divergent: it stops there and is rejected. Gibbs redraws x and y in turn from their conditionals. Every chain starts at the point shown, with no warm-up, and draws from the seed, so a seed gives the same chain for the same settings. The API provides the same computations.

The numbers

The effective sample size is bulk ESS: the chain is split in two halves and rank-normalised, with Geyer's initial monotone sequence. The KS distance is the largest gap between the draws' distribution function and the exact one. The exact marginals are closed forms or one-dimensional integrals (the funnel's x and the smiley's y).
