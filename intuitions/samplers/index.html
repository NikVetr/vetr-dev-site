Funnels, coordinates and the Hastings ratio

A Markov chain Monte Carlo sampler walks through a distribution one step at a time, and in the long run the places it visits are a sample from it. Whether it gets there depends on the distribution's shape in the coordinates the sampler moves in, and on an acceptance rule that is exactly right. Press Play on a panel to watch a chain move; the numbers beside it say how close it has come to the exact answer.

A different sampler from the scripts'. truncated_funnel.R, funnel_test.R and evil_smiley_bug.R sample with Stan's No-U-Turn sampler, which tunes its step size during warm-up and chooses each trajectory's length. This page runs small samplers written for it instead (random-walk Metropolis, Gibbs, and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo with the step size and number of steps you set), with no warm-up and no tuning, so that every step can be watched. Its chains are checked against the exact targets. Stan, run on the same funnel, fails the same way in the centred coordinates (see How it works).

1 One funnel, three coordinates

Neal's funnel is the shape a hierarchical model takes when the spread between its groups is uncertain: y is normal with SD σ, and given y, x is normal with SD ey. Low y is a narrow neck and high y a wide mouth, so no one step size suits both. Sampled in these coordinates (centred), a chain hardly enters the neck: steps sized for the mouth overshoot it, and there HMC's trajectories diverge. Written as two independent standard normals, with x = xraw eσyraw (non-centred), the same distribution is a round blob that a sampler crosses easily. truncated_funnel.R tries a third way: two quantiles in (0, 1) put through Φ⁻¹, which Stan samples on the logit scale.

2 The Hastings ratio

A Metropolis–Hastings step proposes a move from x to x′ and accepts it with probability min(1, π(x′) q(x | x′) / π(x) q(x′ | x)). When proposals are not symmetric, the ratio q(x | x′)/q(x′ | x) keeps the chain on its target. Multiplying a positive parameter by eλZ, a common move for scales and rates, proposes large values more widely than small ones, and its ratio is x′/x. Leave the ratio out, as DoIUnderstandMCMC.R does, and the chain settles on π(x)/x; turn it upside down, as the beta move of simplex_proposal_distributions.R does, and it settles on π(x)/x². Here both coordinates of a log-normal pair are multiplied at each step.

3 A normal and an evil smiley

Two more of the scripts' targets. How it works

The samplers

Random-walk Metropolis proposes a move by a normal step with the SD you set, in both coordinates at once, and accepts it with probability min(1, π(x′)/π(x)). HMC draws a standard normal momentum, follows the target's gradient by the leapfrog method with step size ε for the number of steps you set, and accepts the end point with probability e−ΔH, the change in total energy; when that change passes 1000 (Stan's threshold), the transition is divergent: it stops there and is rejected. Gibbs, as in MCMC_Demo.R, redraws x and y in turn from their conditionals. Every chain starts at the point shown, with no warm-up, and draws from shared/rng.js's mulberry32, so a seed gives the same chain in every browser and in Node. Scripts and agents can run every panel through the API.

The numbers

The effective sample size is posterior's bulk ESS, as Stan and truncated_funnel.R report it: the chain split in two halves, rank-normalised, with Geyer's initial monotone sequence; on R's own series it agrees with posterior::ess_bulk to 10−9. The KS distance is the largest gap between the draws' distribution function and the exact one. The exact marginals are closed forms, or one-dimensional integrals (the funnel's x, the smiley's y) that match R's integrate() to 10−9.

Checked against the exact targets

Long seeded chains, thinned to about one draw per effective sample, pass a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test at 0.1% against every exact marginal: the normal by all three samplers, the non-centred and quantile funnels by HMC and random-walk Metropolis, a mild centred funnel (σ = 0.5), and the smiley by both. The multiplier with its ratio reaches the log-normal; without it, or with it reversed, it passes against the shifted log-normal it settles on and fails against the target by more than ten times the critical value.

The scripts, and Stan

On the same two-dimensional funnel (σ = 1.5, four chains of 1,000 after 1,000 of warm-up), Stan's NUTS diverges on 355 of 4,000 centred transitions and none of its draws fall below y's exact 5% quantile; non-centred and quantile, it has no divergences and 5.3% and 5.4% of its draws there. With the script's σ = 1, 310 centred transitions diverge and 0.7% of draws reach the bottom 5%. So the backend changes how fast a chain fails, not which coordinates fail.

What the scripts do differently

DoIUnderstandMCMC.R multiplies by a normal N(1, 0.01²) draw and accepts without the Hastings ratio, (x/x′) φ((x/x′ − 1)/0.01)/φ((x′/x − 1)/0.01). Rerun as written, its chain on a normal with SD 100 collapses towards 0 (mean 0.0006, against the half-normal's 79.8); with the ratio and a 10% multiplier it reaches 79.7. Its 1% multiplier mixes too slowly to show this in a million steps. This page multiplies by eλZ, whose ratio is exactly x′/x, so the wrong chains' targets have closed forms. simplex_proposal_distributions.R's betaMove returns the log of forward over backward, the ratio upside down: on its Beta(3, 5) example its chain's mean is 0.406, against 0.375 exactly and 0.376 with the ratio corrected. truncated_funnel.R's truncated models do not share one target: its centred and non-centred versions truncate without the per-y normalisation, while its quantile versions normalise x's truncation for each y, and its reference draws mix the two; this page samples only the untruncated funnel. MCMC_Demo.R's Metropolis step moves one coordinate by a uniform window; here random-walk Metropolis moves both by a normal step.
