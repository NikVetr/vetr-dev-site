Pooled or stratified outlier removal

Pool two groups that differ in mean or spread, then drop every value more than z standard deviations from the pooled mean. The gap between the groups inflates the pooled SD, so the rule removes the wrong share of points overall and takes them unevenly from the two groups.

      How it works

      Why pooling changes the cutoff

      The pooled variance is the average variance within the groups plus the spread between their means: with shares wA and wB it is wAσA² + wBσB² + wAwBΔμ². A mean difference therefore widens the pooled cutoffs, and the values beyond them come mostly from whichever group sits further out or spreads wider.

      With equal groups the pooled rule removes too little. A small group far from the majority is different: its whole upper tail can land beyond the pooled cutoff, so pooling removes several times the nominal share from it while barely touching the majority.

      What to do instead

      Standardise within each known group (sex, batch, tissue, site) before applying a z cutoff, so each value is judged against its own group. When the groups are unknown, a cutoff on pooled data mixes outlier detection with group detection.

      Method

      Both groups are normal. Units are group A's SD, with the pooled mean at 0. Every share is an exact normal tail probability, computed in closed form; nothing is simulated. The split into values removed by both rules, by pooling only and by stratifying only comes from the R scripts this page was ported from, written for omics quality control. Their first version dropped part of a wide group's tail and fixed the second group's share at one half; this page follows the corrected version. Scripts and agents can run the same computation through the API.
