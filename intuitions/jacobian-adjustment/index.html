Priors on transformed parameters

In Stan a sampling statement can be written on anything the model computes: put the product of two parameters on the left of a ~ and it reads like a prior on the product. It is not quite one. The statement multiplies the density over the parameters that are actually sampled, and how that density carries over to the product depends on how the product stretches the space. To give the product the distribution written, the model also has to add the log of the transform's Jacobian. And two statements that each give their quantity the right distribution can still make the wrong joint distribution.

  1. With and without the Jacobian
  2. Two marginals are not a joint

1 With and without the Jacobian

Stan samples parameters, here a and b, from the density the model block writes. A statement c ~ p with c = f(a, b) multiplies that density by p(f(a, b)). But changing variables from (a, b) to (a, c) divides a density by |∂c/∂b|, so for a and c to come out as stated the model has to multiply by |∂c/∂b| as well: target += log(abs(…)). Where that factor is a constant, as for sums, leaving it out changes nothing. understanding_Jacobian_adjustments.R tries five transforms, each with its adjustment; choose one, and leave the adjustment out.

2 Two marginals are not a joint

Suppose a and b are meant to be independent, a ~ N(0, 1) and b ~ N(0, s²), so that c = a + b ~ N(0, 1 + s²). jacobian-adjustment_marginal-vs-joint.R writes this as a ~ N(0, 1) and c ~ N(0, √2). Each statement gives its quantity the right distribution and the Jacobian is 1, yet b comes out wrong: statements multiply, so these two make a and c independent, which they were never meant to be. One statement for (a, c) together, with their covariance of 1, gives back what was meant. With a fixed as data, as in the script's later models, the difference is in b's posterior.

How the prior is written

The script's is 1, so c ~ N(0, √2).

How it works

The change of variables

If (a, b) has density p(a, b) and c = f(a, b) is one-to-one in b, then (a, c) has density p(a, b) / |∂c/∂b|. A model whose density over (a, b) is pa(a) pc(f(a, b)) |∂c/∂b| therefore gives (a, c) the density pa(a) pc(c): both as stated, and independent. Without the last factor, (a, c) carries 1/|∂c/∂b| as well. For c = ab that is 1/a, which tilts a log-normal a down by one on the log scale and makes a normal a's integral diverge at 0. Scripts and agents can get every number here through the API.

Computed exactly

Every distribution on this page is a closed form: normals, a Cauchy (the ratio of two independent standard normals), the chi distribution and the χ². The contours of panel 1 hold exactly 50% and 90% of the mass: for a bivariate normal at its Mahalanobis radii, and for the normal product by integrating the standard normal pair (a, ab) over each level set. Panel 2's joint distributions are bivariate normals; with a as data, b's posterior is the stated prior's conditional distribution given a, from which each dot is one seeded draw.

Checked against Stan

Each of the scripts' models was rerun in cmdstan 2.38, four chains of 10,000 draws after 10,000 of warm-up, with the Jacobian as written and left out. The draws' means, second moments, cross moments and quantiles agree with the closed forms here within four Monte Carlo standard errors. Without the Jacobian, the log-normal product's log a averages −0.992 ± 0.008, against −1. The two targets that are not distributions cannot be sampled: without 2a, 34,632 of 40,000 transitions diverge and a collapses to 0; without |a|, a sits at 0 while b's median drifts to −124. With a as data, Stan's posterior means of b are −a for two statements and 0 for one.

What the scripts do differently

For c = a + b and d = a − b, the script adds log|−1| = 0, where the map's determinant is −2. Both are constants, so its draws are the same. Its last transform, d = abc with log|ab|, is the normal product with one more factor, and is left out here. The marginal-vs-joint script's prior on the sample correlation is not ported: its t is r√(n − 2 − r²) by operator precedence, not r√((n − 2)/(1 − r²)); its r is not centred; and the scale it gives the t is that distribution's SD rather than 1. Corrected, the posterior mean of r moves from −0.357 to −0.315.
