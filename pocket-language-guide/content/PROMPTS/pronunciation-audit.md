# Auditing what the pronunciation columns say

The `ipa` column of `data/lang/<code>/*.csv` is generated by `scripts/build_ipa.py`
from the row's `text` (or its romanisation column for the romanised packs), and the
reader's respelling — the "how to say it" line on the board and the italic column on
the card — is derived from that `ipa` by `core/respell.js`, unless a hand-written
override in `data/respell/overrides/` names the concept. So anything in `text` that
is **for the eye rather than for the mouth** ends up spoken: the first report was
`Hello (polite)` respelled for a Spanish reader as *he-lou pe-lait*.

This audit exists to find every class of such material, not every instance: a class
is fixed once, in `spoken()` in `scripts/build_ipa.py` (and its twin `sayable` in
`core/pack.js`), and the column is regenerated. An instance-by-instance fix in the
corpus is wrong here — the labels are wanted on the card.

## Already handled — do not report these

- A **trailing parenthesis**: `Hello (polite)`, `yen (symbol)`, `Forint (Zeichen)`.
  Stripped before transcription.
- A **bracketed slot**: `want to [verb]`, `need [thing]`. Stripped.

## What to look for

Walk `data/lang/<code>/*.csv` for your languages and, for rows with a non-empty
`ipa`, compare `text` with `ipa`. Report a class when the ipa says something the text
does not mean to be said, or says it wrongly. Candidates, none confirmed:

- **Alternatives** joined by a slash — `toilet / WC`, `sie / Ihnen`. Is the slash
  read as a word, silently dropped, or does it split the row into two readings?
- **Fractions and numerals** — `1/2` transcribed as *one slash two*; `0` as a word
  in one language and a digit-name in another; ordinals; times like `14:30`.
- **Currency and other symbols** — `€`, `¥`, `₹`, `%`, `°C`, `+`, `&`. Spoken by
  name, dropped, or left as tofu in the ipa?
- **Abbreviations and initialisms** — `ATM`, `WC`, `SIM`, `PIN`, `Wi-Fi`, `km`,
  `e.g.`, `etc.` Letter by letter, as a word, or expanded? Is it consistent within a
  language, and is the choice the one a speaker of that language makes?
- **Placeholders** — `{}`, `{count}`, `____`, `…`. Does the ipa carry the braces or
  underscores, and does the respelling then print them?
- **Leading or mid-sentence parentheses** — `(Excuse me,) where is…`, `Is it (very)
  spicy?` These are optional *speech*, not labels, and must stay spoken. Report any
  the trailing-parenthesis rule would wrongly catch, i.e. a row whose *last* clause
  is optional speech rather than a label.
- **Editorial marks** — a `*`, a `†`, a `(sic)`, a script-name note.
- Anything else where the respelling a reader sees would make them say something the
  stranger will not understand.

## How to report

Write findings to `tmp/pronunciation-audit-<your range>.md` **as you go** — one
section per class, each with: the languages it occurs in, the count per language, two
or three example rows (`concept_id`, `text`, `ipa`), what the row *should* say, and a
proposed rule stated precisely enough to implement in `spoken()` (or, where the
answer differs by language, a table of what each language does). Say which classes
you checked and found clean. Do not edit any corpus file, any script, or run any
build; the coordinating session applies rules and regenerates the column once.

Price a class by its reach: a rule that changes 1,000 cells across 48 languages is a
different decision from one that touches four rows of Klingon, and the report should
make that visible before anyone acts on it.
