## Notes from the verifier

- **AO Angola** — SEEDED VALUE WRONG. 113 police and 115 ambulance are both stale, and 115 was fire rather than ambulance in the first place. Angolan press (Novo Jornal, Ver Angola, May 2020) reported the police deactivated terminal 113 in favour of 111; the Serviço de Protecção Civil e Bombeiros told state broadcaster RNA on 2024-11-28 that 'o terminal 115 foi desactivado, e o 111 é agora o contacto que os cidadãos devem ligar em caso de qualquer emergência', covering medical, police and fire. Portugal's foreign ministry lists '111 – Centro Integrado de Segurança Pública (CISP) – número de emergência nacional'. Angola's Ministry of Interior promotes 111 as reaching National Police, Bombeiros and other services. UNRESOLVED CONFLICT worth flagging: Canada's advisory still lists the legacy set (police 113, medical 116, firefighters 115) and the UK FCDO still says 112 — no Angolan source endorses either. Also note CISP is rolled out province by province (confirmed operational in Luanda, Huambo since 2021-22, Benguela 2024, Cabinda), so 111 is the national number by designation but coverage tracks CISP presence.
- **AR Argentina** — SEEDED VALUE CORRECTED. 911 is genuinely nationwide - Ley 25.367 art. 2 gives the Sistema de Emergencias Coordinadas 'un unico numero de telefono de tres cifras (*911), que sera el mismo en todo el pais' (https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/65000-69999/65412/norma.htm), and the code 911 was assigned under the Plan Fundamental de Numeracion Nacional. But 'all services' overclaims: the national government's own page scopes 911 as 'Central de Emergencias Nacional - Llamalos si necesitas pedir una ambulancia de emergencias o presencia de la policia', and lists 100 Bomberos separately for fires. Hence the split cell. 107 SAME was deliberately excluded: the same page limits it to 'Ciudad de Buenos Aires y localidades de la provincia de Buenos Aires', so it is not nationwide. 101 was also excluded - the Buenos Aires city government's own useful-numbers page no longer lists it and gives 911 as the 'Linea directa de la Policia de la Ciudad' (https://buenosaires.gob.ar/gcaba_historico/jefaturadegabinete/telefonos-utiles). Medical dispatch is the one thing that genuinely varies by province (107 in many, 911 in Buenos Aires province, 106 in Neuquen), which is why the cell names 911 rather than a medical short code. Currency ARS confirmed against ISO 4217 List One (numeric 032).
- **BR Brazil** — Seeded numbers confirmed correct; only the order changed so ambulance precedes fire. ANATEL, the federal telecom regulator, publishes the authoritative table of SERVICOS PUBLICOS DE EMERGENCIA codes: 190 Policia Militar, 191 Policia Rodoviaria Federal, 192 'Servico de Atendimento Medico de Urgencia -SAMU', 193 Corpo de Bombeiros, 199 Defesa Civil - and states that its 2004 Regulamento sobre as Condicoes de Acesso e Fruicao dos Servicos de Utilidade Publica 'uniformizou, em todo o Brasil, os codigos dos servicos de emergencia', with all emergency calls free. Confirmed operationally by the Distrito Federal public security secretariat, whose CIOB coordinates 190, 192, 193, 199, 112 and 911 (https://www.ssp.df.gov.br/emergencia-190-193-e-199/). Brazil genuinely has no single number, so all three earn their space and 199 Defesa Civil was the entry cut. Worth flagging for the coordinator: the same ANATEL table carries a row 'Policia Militar - SOMENTE CELULAR' against the codes '112/911', so 112 and 911 are formally assigned to reach the Military Police from mobile handsets nationwide - a real local assignment, not GSM fallback. It was left off the card only for space, and would be the natural fourth entry ('112 also works') if the renderer can take it. ANATEL also lists 128 'Servicos de Emergencia no Mercosul' as 'servico nao ativado' - do not print 128. Currency BRL confirmed against ISO 4217 List One (numeric 986).
- **BY Belarus** — Seeded '112 all services' confirmed and expanded. BelTA quoting the MChS press secretary: 112 is 'номер вызова единой диспетчерской службы МЧС, как и 101', works from the mobiles of all operators in the country and from landlines, and 'все остальные номера экстренных служб (101, 102, 103, 104) сохраняются'. Minsk Oblast MChS confirms the ЕДДС routes onward to 102/103/104 ('Запомните! Вызов ЕДДС – по телефону «112»', https://minobl.mchs.gov.by/novosti/291581/). Number-to-service mapping (101/112 MChS, 102 militia, 103 ambulance, 104 gas; emergency calls free even on a blocked account) from the national operator MTS Belarus, https://www.mts.by/help/mobilnaya-svyaz/golosovaya-svyaz/kak-zvonit-na-nomera-ekstrennykh-sluzhb/. 101 omitted from the cell because Belarusian 112 is the MChS line itself. Currency: BYN is correct - BYR (974) was withdrawn 2017-01 per ISO 4217 list-three. mchs.gov.by and mvd.gov.by both block datacentre IPs, so the МЧС home page itself could not be read directly.
- **CD DR Congo** — SEEDED VALUE REFUTED — do not restore it. Government of Canada, verbatim: 'There is no centralized number to reach emergency services. Research and carry contact information for local police and medical facilities.' UK FCDO publishes no DRC emergency number either. 112 does exist as a Congolese National Police call-centre line, but 7sur7.cd (2026-04-24) reported the centre being relaunched with government and ARPTC support 'twelve years after its initial launch', alongside green numbers 126 and 164, with coverage centred on Kinshasa; MONUSCO's 112 awareness work (2017) was likewise Kinshasa-only, in three communes. 117 is also cited in Congolese press. Cell emptied rather than left asserting a nationwide number that does not exist — core/pack.js line 191 already returns null for an empty value, so nothing prints. Currency CDF confirmed.
- **CH Switzerland** — Seeded set was right; I reordered to ch.ch's own summary box (ambulance, police, fire) which also matches the reviewed FR row's pattern. ch.ch: '112 ... connects you with the police emergency control centre ... You can also call this number using a foreign SIM card or when using a prepaid mobile phone card with no credit.' The AEFV emergency numbers are 112/117/118/144 plus 143/145/147, quoted in the Organisation Notrufe Schweiz steering-committee letter of 15.07.2024 (https://www.144.ch/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/202407116_Infomationsschreiben_Notrufnummer_DE.pdf), which also confirms a 112 call is forwarded to the competent centre with accepted time loss and asks bodies NOT to promote 112. 1414 (Rega, https://www.rega.ch/en/emergency-number-1414) deliberately omitted: it is not an AEFV emergency number, does not appear on ch.ch, 144 alerts Rega anyway, and both Rega and the Swiss Alpine Club (https://www.sac-cas.ch/de/die-alpen/nothilfenummern-richtig-anfordern-hilfe-per-natel-in-den-schweizer-bergen-17722/) tell you to fall back to 112 if 1414 cannot be reached, because only 112 roams onto any available network. Suggest carrying 1414 in the hiking section instead. Also note 145 poison and, in Valais, air rescue run by operators other than Rega - both out of scope for one line.
- **CI Côte d'Ivoire** — UK FCDO gives police '110, 111 or 170', fire '180', ambulance '185'. The French embassy in Abidjan (ci.diplomatie.gouv.fr/en-cas-durgence) gives an identical set. The official Ivorian government services directory annuaire.gouv.ci confirms 180 for pompiers and 185. Seeded 111 police and 180 fire both confirmed; 185 ambulance added. 110 and 170 also reach police per both ministries. policenationale-ci.com states 'Le numéro d'urgence de la Police nationale est le 170' but is a police information magazine on a .com domain with a gmail contact address, not an official government site, so it was not used to override the seed. No authority found for 112 answering in Côte d'Ivoire.
- **CL Chile** — Seeded numbers confirmed correct; only the order changed so ambulance precedes fire. Chile's official visitor-information page under 'Condiciones de seguridad' lists verbatim 'Servicio de Atencion Medico de Urgencias (SAMU) 131 / Bomberos 132. / Carabineros 133 / PDI: 134 / Cuerpo de Socorro Andino 136 / Busqueda y salvamento maritimo 137 / Servicio de Busqueda y Salvamento Aereo 138 / Informaciones policiales 139'. Independently confirmed by Chile's consular service (https://www.consulado.gob.cl/emergencias-en-chile) with the same 131/132/133 assignment. Answering the specific question about 130: it exists and is real - CONAF's National Forest Fire Prevention Campaign site publishes '130 | 132 | 133 | 134' for reporting forest fires (https://www.conafprevencionincendios.cl/) - but it is a forest-fire reporting line for fuego en areas silvestres, it is absent from both of Chile's official visitor/consular emergency lists, and a traveler in trouble should dial 132 for any fire that concerns them. So the current general-emergency set is 131/132/133 and 130 is excluded. There is no single consolidated number in Chile. Currency CLP confirmed against ISO 4217 List One (numeric 152).
- **CO Colombia** — Seeded value confirmed correct. The Policia Nacional describes 123 as 'la linea unica de emergencias Nacional', operating 24/7 and letting citizens 'acceder a todos los servicios de emergencia y seguridad que ofrece el Estado gratuitamente'. Corroborated by the Departamento Administrativo de la Funcion Publica FAQ titled 'Linea unica de emergencias Nacional 123' (https://www1.funcionpublica.gov.co/preguntas-frecuentes/-/asset_publisher/sqxafjubsrEu/content/linea-unica-de-emergencias-nacional-123/28585938). Answering the specific question: it is nationwide as a number, not city-by-city, but the depth of consolidation behind it is city-by-city - the CRC/MinTIC framework (Decreto 25 de 2002, Decreto 1078 de 2015, Resolucion CRC 5050 de 2016) assigns 123 to territorial entities that establish a Centro de Atencion de Emergencias, so in Bogota, Medellin and Cali it is a true NUSE fusing police, fire, ambulance, mobility and health, while in smaller municipalities it lands at the police. Either way the caller reaches help, so 'all services' is fair. 155 (women victims of violence) is a guidance line, not an emergency line, and is off the card. Currency COP confirmed against ISO 4217 List One (numeric 170).
- **KE Kenya** — Both official police sites publish the same set: nationalpolice.go.ke states 'Police Hotline: 999 / 112 / 911' and kenyapolice.go.ke states 'Police Hot Lines - 999 / 112 / 911', described as available 24/7. All three answer nationwide, so all three are listed — 911 is worth the characters because a US traveller will reach for it reflexively and in Kenya it works. Seeded value was correct; 911 added. Both sites have expired/invalid TLS certificates and had to be fetched with certificate verification disabled.
- **KG Kyrgyzstan** — Seeded value confirmed exactly as it stands. The Kyrgyz MChS publishes 112 as its '112 Единая диспетчерская служба' on its current contacts page and in its site footer, and the Cabinet of Ministers' record of the launch quotes the MChS minister: 'Теперь каждый житель страны ... сможет воспользоваться единой службой, просто набрав номер «112»' and 'Система «112» объединит все дежурно-диспетчерские службы («101», «102», «103», «104»)' (https://www.gov.kg/ru/post/s/abdyirahman-mamataliev-sluzhba-112-yavlyaetsya-odnim-iz-vazhnyih-napravleniy-obespecheniya-bezopasnosti-stranyi-pozvolyayushhih-sokratit-kolichestvo-zhertv-i-postradavshih-pri-chrezvyichaynyi). Calls are answered by MChS's Crisis Management Centre operators, so this is the local service answering, not GSM fallback. I did NOT add 102/103: every Kyrgyz government page that maps each digit to a service was unreachable (mvd.gov.kg presents a certificate for e.mvd.kg and 404s on every path, 103.gov.kg returned 502, med.kg reset the connection, cbd.minjust.gov.kg is a JS app), and search snippets alone are not a source. Worth a second look by someone who can reach those hosts - 102 police and 103 ambulance would probably earn a place.
- **KZ Kazakhstan** — Seeded '112 all services' confirmed and expanded. The ЕДДС '112' exists in law 'на территории Республики Казахстан' (Order of the Minister of Internal Affairs No. 138 of 23.02.2015, Rules re-issued by MChS order No. 369 of 28.08.2025) and dispatches fire, law-enforcement, ambulance and utility services. egov.kz's official article adds that calls are accepted from any oblast, city or settlement from any landline or mobile and are free, and that 112 does not replace 101/102/103 (https://egov.kz/cms/ru/articles/emergency_number_112 - the page is a JS app, so its text was read via the search index rather than fetched directly). 103 ambulance is fixed by the Rules on emergency medical care ('Все вызовы от граждан при нарушении состояния здоровья принимаются по номеру «103»', https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V1700015473) and 102 police by the MVD portal (https://polisia.kz/ru/politsiya-napominaet-o-pravilah-vyzova-102-i-otvetstvennosti/). MChS has been merging 101 into 112 (Astana), so 101 is not worth printing.
- **MX Mexico** — Seeded value confirmed correct. The IFT acuerdo published in the Diario Oficial states in its Considerando Septimo 'se determina que el numero unico de emergencia sea el 911' and that the 'Lineamiento establece como numero unico el 911'. The Secretariado Ejecutivo del Sistema Nacional de Seguridad Publica site (https://www.gob.mx/911) presents 911 as the unified nationwide system and itself published the retirement notice 'Recuerda que los codigos de emergencias 060, 061, 065, 066, 068 y 080 dejaran de funcionar en el mes de junio' (published 26 May 2017). Answering the specific question: the old numbers do NOT still answer - they were deactivated across all 32 federal entities between June and July 2017, after a staged rollout that reached 16 states in October 2016; Mexico City redirected 066 to 911 from 9 January 2017. 089 remains live but is the anonymous-tip line, not an emergency line, so it is off the card. Currency MXN confirmed against ISO 4217 List One (numeric 484).
- **MZ Mozambique** — SEEDED SERVICE LABEL WRONG: 198 is the Bombeiros (fire brigade), not ambulance. Mozambican government listing (chigubo.gov.mz) gives 'PRM Emergência - 119' and 'Bombeiros Emergência - 197-198'. Portugal's foreign ministry gives PRM as '119, 112'; the official tourism site fikani.visitmozambique.gov.mz lists POLÍCIA as '112 /122' and BOMBEIROS as '82198'; Canada gives police as 112. So 119 and 112 both reach police — 119 led because Mozambique's own government publishes it. Mozambique has no public ambulance short code: UK FCDO points to 848888/823388 and Canada to private provider Aeromed (84911), so no ambulance entry is claimed. Nuance for the coordinator: from a mobile the fire brigade may need an operator prefix (82198 on the Mcel/Vodacom pattern, 800198198 given for Maputo by Portugal's MNE), so bare 198 is the fixed-line form.
- **PE Peru** — SEEDED VALUE CORRECTED - the seeded cell presented 106 as the nationwide ambulance number and it is not. The Peruvian state platform gob.pe lists under the heading 'Principales numeros de emergencia a nivel nacional' only: Central policial 105, Policia de carreteras 110, Infosalud 113, Defensa Civil 115, Bomberos 116, Denuncia contra la violencia familiar y sexual 100. SAMU 106 appears under a separate heading that reads 'Para atencion en las regiones: Amazonas, Ancash, Ayacucho, Huancavelica, Huanuco, Junin, La Libertad, Loreto, Madre de Dios, Piura, Lima Region, Tacna, Tumbes, Ucayali, Lambayeque y Pasco; puedes llamar al 106' - which excludes Cusco, Arequipa, Puno, Ica, Cajamarca, Apurimac, Moquegua and San Martin, i.e. most of the tourist circuit outside Lima. MINSA's 2025-2026 expansion reporting still names the same 16 regions. Printing '106 ambulance' would therefore hand a traveler in Cusco a number that may not answer, so it was dropped from the cell. 116 is justified as 'fire and ambulance' from the fire service's own live dispatch record, headed 'EMERGENCIAS 116', which shows AMB- ambulance units dispatched to 'EMERGENCIA MEDICA / TRASLADOS', 'EMERGENCIA MEDICA / TRAUMATICAS' and 'ACCIDENTE VEHICULAR' (https://sgonorte.bomberosperu.gob.pe/24horas). Answering the specific question about 911: do not print it. As of 2026 the Central de Emergencias 911 is a Lima/Callao pilot live in only five districts (Brena, Jesus Maria, Magdalena del Mar, Pueblo Libre, San Miguel), with the central building due December 2026; it will eventually absorb 105, 116, 106 and 100. Currency PEN confirmed against ISO 4217 List One (numeric 604); note the register now names it simply 'Sol'.
- **RU Russia** — Seeded numbers all correct; I dropped 101 fire only to keep the line short, because 112 is itself the MChS-run unified line and reaches fire directly. MChS: 112 is the single number for fire, emergency response, police, ambulance, gas and anti-terror; free; dialable with no credit and with no SIM; searches for any available network; 'не заменяет существующие номера ... 01, 02, 03, 04 со стационарного телефона и ... 101, 102, 103 и 104 – с мобильного'. 101 = fire and rescue is confirmed by MChS's own site footer ('01 или 101 Единый телефон пожарных и спасателей', https://42.mchs.gov.ru/deyatelnost/poleznaya-informaciya/pamyatka-dlya-naseleniya-po-sisteme-112). Legal basis Government Decree No. 958 of 21.11.2011 (https://mchs.gov.ru/dokumenty/623); commissioning was staged region by region (e.g. https://51.mchs.gov.ru/deyatelnost/poleznaya-informaciya/sistema-112/sistema-112), which is why the direct ambulance and police lines stay on the card.
